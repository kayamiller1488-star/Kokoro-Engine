# Voice latency baseline — 2026-11-22

The reference numbers for "how fast does she answer", broken down by stage so a future change
of mouth, brain or ear can be compared against something real instead of a feeling.

Measured on the machine that runs the character, with the game **not** competing for the GPU
unless noted. Every number below is wall-clock from a live service, not an estimate.

## Stack at the time of measurement

| piece | what it was |

| Ear | SenseVoice-small int8 (CPU, 16 kHz) for English; faster-whisper `large-v3-turbo` (CUDA fp16) for Japanese, port 9882 |

## Stage timings

| stage | median | spread | how it was measured | varies with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| End-of-speech detection (VAD) | **650 ms** | fixed | `SILENCE_S = 0.65` in the mod: it waits this long after the last loud frame before closing the utterance | nothing — it is a constant. Lowering it trades responsiveness for clipped last syllables |
| Ear — English (SenseVoice) | **146 ms** | 128–230 | POST of a 1.8 s WAV to `:9882/transcribe`, 8 samples | audio length, CPU load |
| Ear — Japanese pinned (whisper, warm) | **189 ms** | 171–300 | same, `?language=ja` | audio length, GPU contention |
| Ear — auto + whisper second opinion | **318 ms** | 303–336 | same, romanised-Japanese audio (SenseVoice pass + whisper pass) | only fires when the first pass looks Japanese-ish |
| Ear — whisper cold start | **23–34 s** | once per ear start | logged `whisper warmed up in …` | skipped entirely when < 6 GB VRAM is free |
| Brain — full reply | **280 ms** | 262–651 | `/v1/chat/completions`, 80 max tokens, her real persona prompt, ~22 tokens out | prompt length, reply length |
| Brain — first token | **101 ms** | — | same, `stream: true` | prompt length |
| App orchestration + brain (text in → reply stored) | **979 ms** | 801–2628 (p90 1418) | consecutive `user`/`assistant` rows in `conversation_messages`, last 19 turns | memory retrieval, emotion tagging, conversation writes — this is the ~700 ms that is *not* the model |
| Mouth — short Japanese (6 chars) | **105 ms** | — | full WAV fetch from `:9881`, 6 samples | text length |
| Mouth — 20 chars Japanese | **147 ms** | — | same | text length |
| Mouth — 24 chars English | **295 ms** | — | same | text length, first call after a voice change |

## End to end

Directly measured inside the app (text submitted → her first audio sample, 5 turns):
**1906 ms median** (997–2428).

Composed from the stages for a spoken Japanese turn:

```
VAD 650 ms  +  ear 189 ms  +  orchestration+brain 979 ms  +  mouth 150 ms  ≈  1.97 s
```

Both agree: **about 2 seconds from the moment you stop talking to the moment she starts.**
Of that, the models are only ~0.6 s; the remaining ~1.4 s is fixed waiting (0.65 s VAD) and the
app's own orchestration (~0.7 s).

## What this means for swapping parts later

- A faster **mouth** can win at most ~0.15 s — it is not the bottleneck.
- A faster **brain** can win at most ~0.28 s on the full reply (~0.10 s to first token), unless the
  shell is switched to speak per sentence while generating, which would hide most of it.
- A better **ear** costs +0.05 to +0.17 s versus SenseVoice and buys accuracy on accented
  Japanese; that is the only stage where paying more time has clearly paid off so far.
- The two biggest single wins left are not models at all: shortening `SILENCE_S` (0.65 s) and
  finding the ~0.7 s inside the app between receiving a transcript and calling the brain.

## Re-measuring

`baseline/measure-latency.py` reproduces the stage table (ear, brain, mouth) in ~30 s. The app-side
turn and the orchestration figure come from the CDP probe in the same script's docstring and from
the `conversation_messages` timestamps respectively.

Caveat worth repeating: numbers taken while a game holds the GPU are the game's numbers first.
Measure idle, and say which state you measured.
